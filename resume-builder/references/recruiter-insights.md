# What Big-Tech Recruiters and Hiring Managers Look For

These are widely shared, publicly discussed practices from recruiters and hiring managers at large tech companies. Processes change; treat them as orientation, not guarantees.

## Universal first-skim (6–10 seconds)
Recruiters typically scan: current/most recent title and company → dates (tenure, progression, gaps) → education → skills keywords matching the req → one or two impressive bullets. Make these findable without reading:
- Bold titles and companies; dates aligned consistently.
- Most impressive, relevant, quantified bullet first in each role.
- Skills near the top for technical roles.
- No dense paragraphs; whitespace between roles.

Strong positive signals across companies: scope and scale (users, traffic, money), ownership ("led", "designed", "owned"), promotions, measurable outcomes, recognisable tech relevant to the role, and clear progression.

Red flags: walls of text, duty-only bullets, vague claims ("improved performance"), long skill lists with no evidence, typos, inconsistent dates, unexplained job-hopping (several <1-year stints), 3-page US resumes, photos/personal data in markets that don't expect them, obviously AI-generated filler language ("spearheaded synergistic initiatives", "leveraged cutting-edge").

## Google
- XYZ formula ("Accomplished X as measured by Y by doing Z") — publicly recommended by Google's own recruiters and former SVP People Ops.
- Values: impact at scale, technical depth, "Googleyness" (collaboration, comfort with ambiguity), leadership without authority.
- Role level matters: for L5+ show design ownership and cross-team influence; for L6+ multi-team/organisational impact.
- Recruiters search their ATS by skills and titles — mirror the posting's terms.

## Amazon / AWS
- Interviews are built on the 16 Leadership Principles (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Invent and Simplify, Are Right A Lot, Learn and Be Curious, Hire and Develop the Best, Insist on the Highest Standards, Think Big, Bias for Action, Frugality, Earn Trust, Dive Deep, Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, Deliver Results, Strive to be Earth's Best Employer, Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility).
- Resume bullets that already read like compressed STAR stories map well to these. Show customer impact, data-backed decisions, cost savings (Frugality), and delivery under constraints. Don't name-drop the principles; demonstrate them.
- Metrics-heavy culture: numbers matter a lot.
- SDE levels: SDE I (entry), SDE II (mid, owns features), SDE III/Senior (owns systems, leads projects), Principal (org-wide).

## Meta
- "Impact" is the core currency — what changed for users or the business. Engineers are evaluated on impact, so bullets should state outcomes (engagement, latency, reliability, revenue, developer productivity).
- Move-fast execution and scale: big user numbers, shipping cadence, experimentation (A/B tests).
- E4/E5/E6 progression mirrors scope: feature → project → multi-team.

## Microsoft
- Growth mindset, collaboration, customer outcomes. Shows well through cross-team work, learning new domains, and customer/enterprise impact.
- Azure / enterprise scale keywords matter for cloud roles.

## Apple
- Craft, attention to detail, deep domain expertise, cross-functional work with design/hardware. Concise, precise, polished resumes. Secrecy culture — don't overshare proprietary detail about current employers either.

## Netflix / high-autonomy cultures
- Senior, judgment-heavy; show independent ownership of high-stakes decisions and context-setting rather than task lists.

## Startups / scale-ups
- Breadth, speed, ownership end to end, wearing multiple hats, shipping to real customers, 0→1 products. Founding/early-employee experience is valued; side projects with real users count.

## Senior / Staff / Lead engineers
- Lead with scope: systems designed, engineers led/influenced, business-critical outcomes.
- Show technical leadership artefacts: design docs, architecture decisions, standards adopted org-wide, platforms others build on, incident leadership, hiring/interviewing, mentoring.
- 2 pages acceptable even in the US if dense with relevant impact.
- "Lead SDE" titles: clarify whether people management is included; ICs should emphasise technical leadership, managers should emphasise team outcomes and people growth.

## Referrals and recruiters
A referral gets a human look; the resume still has to do its job in seconds. Recommend the person tailors even for referred applications.

## r/EngineeringResumes consensus (useful defaults)
- One page unless 10+ years relevant experience; single column; no photo; no objective; minimal colour.
- Skills section concise; avoid listing every tool.
- Bullets: action + technical specifics + impact; avoid "responsible for".
- Projects matter for students/new grads; link GitHub only if it's tidy.
- Don't use "ATS score" websites as a target; they're not what employers' systems do.
- Templates often recommended: a clean LaTeX template (e.g., Jake's Resume style) or an equivalent plain Word/Google Docs layout — the `build_resume.py` output follows this style.
